# Analysis — week 7

Ancestor agreement falls fastest at the high rate and slowest at the low rate, as expected from a per-position independent substitution model — but the point of this pack is not that ranking, it is what the three runs at each rate do and do not show.

## What this model omits

This model has no selection: every substitution is equally likely to survive, so nothing here favours a functional sequence over a broken one. It has no population structure: one lineage is copied forward, with no branching, no drift between subpopulations and no bottlenecks. Three seeds per rate show that runs vary, not what the true variance is — a nine-run sample cannot establish a threshold, an error rate at which reconstruction reliably fails, that would hold for a real population or for any other week's copying medium. Treat every number here as a property of this specific simplified model, not as a biological measurement.

## A conclusion this pack cannot justify

That there exists a single copying-error rate above which ancestry becomes 'unreadable' in general. The three rates here were chosen to illustrate a trend, not to bracket a threshold, and no biological selection or population process is represented, so no claim about a real lineage's error tolerance follows from this pack.
