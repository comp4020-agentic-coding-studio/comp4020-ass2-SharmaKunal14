# The poor image

For three weeks you have known what was done to the copy. This hour is
about the ordinary case, where you do not.

---

## The image that travels

Steyerl's argument: the low-resolution copy is not a degraded version of
something better, but the form in which images actually circulate.

*From Hito Steyerl, "In Defense of the Poor Image",* e-flux journal #10
(2009), free at e-flux.com.*

---

## The narrower claim, and it's ours

A circulating image may arrive without the metadata, logs or intermediate
files needed to reconstruct its history.

Sometimes it arrives with them. That weaker situation — evidence thinner
than the question — is what this week works in.

## Both fit

Apply the tolerance — both land inside it on every measurement.

The file has exactly one history. These five observations

## The sentence to get right

"These two routes are indistinguishable" is incomplete, and almost
certainly false as stated.

What is true: **they are indistinguishable on these measurements, at this
tolerance.**

---

## A claim about the evidence, not the objects

Change the tolerance — it may dissolve.

Add a measurement — it may dissolve.

Say which measurements and which tolerance, every time.

---

## Ask for evidence before you get it

Name an observation that would discriminate — **before** obtaining it.

Look first and decide what it means after, and whatever you saw will
support whichever route you preferred.

---

## Three records, one distinction

| Record | Separates the routes? |
|---|---|
| Working-directory listing | No — co-existence is not descent |
| Handover note | No — same gap, human direction |
| Transformation record (input hash matches) | **Yes** |

---

## Ruling out is not establishing

Suppose the transformation record excludes route A.

You have not shown route B happened — only that of the two histories
proposed, one is excluded. Others fit the same record shape.

The claim you can defend: **not route A, and consistent with route B.**

## Text walkthrough

For a reader who cannot use the deck view: Output 7 arrives with five
observations — dimensions, file size, chroma subsampling, and the absence
of EXIF — and two proposed processing histories both fit them within the
stated tolerance, so the initial evidence underdetermines which one
happened. The key sentence to get right is that "these routes are
indistinguishable" is incomplete on its own; what is actually true is that
they are indistinguishable on these particular measurements at this
particular tolerance, and changing either could separate them. Of three
further evidence items available, two — a working-directory listing
showing a full-size file existed, and a handover note confirming one
existed somewhere — fail to separate the routes because co-existence is
not descent: an interim file could be unrelated. A transformation record
that names the full-size file as the actual input to the resize, with a
matching hash, does separate them, because it records what read what
rather than merely what existed. Even having excluded one route this way,
the evidence has not established the other — any history of the same shape
would produce the same record — so the defensible claim is "not route A,
and consistent with route B," which is weaker than "it was route B."
