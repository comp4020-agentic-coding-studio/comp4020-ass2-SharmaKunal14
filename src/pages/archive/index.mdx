# Class Copy Archive

Everything this course argues about comes from here. Each workshop deposits a
result into one of these collections, and later weeks reuse what earlier weeks
left behind.

Two rules govern the archive, and they are the same two rules the course is
about.

**Ancestry stays inside a collection.** A recording is not descended from a
typed page because the two were studied in consecutive weeks. Relationships
between collections are **comparisons**, and are labelled as such: week 7
compares the limits of ancestry inference with week 6's text reconstruction.
It does not claim a sequence descended from a manuscript.

**Known and inferred are recorded separately.** A parent we logged when we made
the copy is a fact about the archive. A parent we worked out from shared
features is a conclusion, and it carries the evidence and the uncertainty that
produced it.

## Recurring collections

### Print

Two documents from the same fictional copy room, and everything copied from
them. Week 1 establishes the measurement discipline and the exact-copy control;
week 5 starts a branching hand-copied text; week 6 reconstructs it.

They are deliberately two documents rather than one. If week 5 branched from the
card students already read in week 1, week 6 would be reconstructing a text they
could recall, and the exercise would test memory instead of evidence.

| ID | Item | Source | Operation | Used in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `print/card` | Copy room test card 0S1I-B85l | Authored; four lines built from confusable glyph pairs | none — this is the reference | 1 |
| `print/card-control` | Digital control, 8 copy operations | `print/card`, parent known | copied byte for byte eight times, checksummed at each step, so it is comparable with the eight-operation photocopy | 1 |
| `print/card-g1` | Photocopy, 1 copy operation | `print/card`, parent known | one photocopy pass, fixed machine and settings | 1 |
| `print/card-g4` | Photocopy, 4 copy operations | `print/card-g3`, parent known, not published | copy of the previous generation, same settings | 1 |
| `print/card-g8` | Photocopy, 8 copy operations | `print/card-g7`, parent known, not published | copy of the previous generation, same settings | 1 |
| `print/ordinance` | Ordinance of the Copy-House | Authored; twelve numbered lines | none — withheld until week 6's reveal | 5, 6 |
| `print/ordinance-a` to `-e` | Five witnesses | `print/ordinance`, parents known and withheld | hand copying in a branching assignment | 5, 6 |

Each copy of the card is published as **two readers' transcriptions** rather than
as an image of damaged print. The measurement is a comparison of texts, so an
image would add an eyesight test this course refuses to grade, and it would make
the accessible route a lesser version of the task instead of the task itself.

Every card copy is an authored teaching scenario, labelled as such in its own
file, rather than a recording of a real machine and real readers.

Accessibility alternative for this collection: plain text throughout, one
labelled line per row, every transcription the same length as its source.

### Audio

A thirty-second source recording and everything dubbed from it. Week 2 asks
which shared features are evidence of a relationship; week 3 keeps the same
source and asks how a claim about it would be tested.

| ID | Item | Source | Operation | Used in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `audio/source` | Source recording, 30 seconds | Authored | none — this is the reference | 2, 3 |
| `audio/w` | Copy W | `audio/source`, parent known | dubbed directly from the source, deck 2 | 2 |
| `audio/x` | Copy X | lost intermediate, parent inferred | dubbed from a copy that already carried the 00:12.400 dropout, deck 1 | 2 |
| `audio/y` | Copy Y | lost intermediate, parent inferred | dubbed from the same copy as X, deck 2 | 2 |
| `audio/z` | Copy Z | `audio/source`, parent known | dubbed directly from the source, deck 1 | 2 |

The relationship between X and Y is recorded as **inferred**, with the feature
that supports it and the assumption it rests on. The parents of W and Z are
**known**, because the log records them. Those are different kinds of entry and
the archive does not merge them.

X and Z share a high-frequency rolloff because they were dubbed on the same deck.
That is recorded as an equipment note, not as an edge. Shared equipment is not
shared ancestry, and an archive that filed it as ancestry would be asserting a
relationship nobody has evidence for.

No recordings are published. The evidence is the feature table, so the inference
does not depend on hearing and the non-listening route is the route. Every item
is an authored teaching scenario, labelled as such in its own file.

Accessibility alternative for this collection: feature tables in plain text, with
locations as timestamps and presence stated per recording.

### Image

An authored source image, 2400 by 1600, and the outputs produced from it. Week 4
asks whether the evidence on an output can determine its history; week 10 asks
whether a restoration of it matches the original; week 11 asks what a provenance
record establishes and what it omits.

| ID | Item | Source | Operation | Used in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `image/source` | Authored source image, 2400 x 1600 | Authored | none — this is the reference | 4, 10, 11 |
| `image/output-7` | Output 7, 1200 x 800 | **inferred**: consistent with a save, resize and second save; a single-save route is excluded by a retained transformation record naming a full-size JPEG as the resize input, with a matching hash | see the week 4 route cards | 4, 10, 11 |

Output 7's parent is recorded as **inferred, and partially**. A retained transformation
record names a full-size JPEG as the input to the resize, with a matching hash,
which excludes any route that resizes the source directly. The working-directory
listing retained alongside it does not support that conclusion on its own: it
shows the files co-existed, not that one was read to make the other. It does not
establish the route that was proposed: every history that saved at full size
before resizing would leave the same listing. Ruling out is not establishing,
and the record says which of the two it is doing.

The archive holds two records of the same events that differ in what they
captured: a listing of what existed, and a record of what read what. Only the
second supports an ancestry claim. A listing that is genuine, relevant and
insufficient is the ordinary condition of a provenance record, and week 11
returns to it.

Two evidence items a reader might expect are absent: no EXIF survives, and no
upload log was retained. Their absence is recorded rather than left as a gap.

Accessibility alternative for this collection: observations as property-value
tables, routes as numbered written steps, and every diagram accompanied by the
same information in text.

## Model studies

These three are **simplified models**, not specimens. Each exists to test one
idea under stated parameters, and each page names what the model leaves out.

### Sequence model

A synthetic replication model with declared error rates and fixed seeds, used in
week 7 to watch ancestry inference degrade. A 24-symbol ancestor over a
four-symbol alphabet is copied forward for 40 generations at three fixed error
rates (low 0.02, medium 0.08, high 0.2 per position), three fixed seeds per
rate, with agreement against the generation-0 ancestor reported at generations
1, 5, 10, 20 and 40. Settings and seeds are published for reproduction in
`/packs/week-07/settings.json`. It omits selection and population structure,
so it demonstrates a limit of inference rather than a biological result.

### Bits

A fixed short bitstring, used in week 8 to price error correction in overhead
rather than in good intentions. A 3x repetition code (payload `1011`,
codeword `111 000 111 111`) is worked by hand: majority-vote decoding
corrects one bit-flip per triplet at 200% overhead, and a second flip inside
the same triplet produces a wrong, unflagged decode — the guarantee's stated
limit, not an edge case outside it.

### Distribution model

A small categorical distribution resampled from its own output, used in week 9.
It is categorical resampling, not a trained language model, and the page says so.

## What a record holds

Every specimen record carries an identifier, its collection, its source and
rights, the operation and settings that produced it, measurements with units,
an accessible alternative, and the teaching weeks that use it. Known parents and
inferred parents are separate fields.

Specimens are added as each workshop is built. Instructor examples are labelled
as instructor examples; there is no fabricated student work here.
