## 1. Purpose and progression

**Distinct capability:** Construct competing compatible histories and request evidence that could distinguish them.

**Archive placement:** Image. Use week 3 measurement discipline. Establish a separate image family and preserve source and logs for weeks 10–11. Prepare students to allow unresolved text relationships in week 6.

## 2. Weekly teaching brief

Use week 4 dates from the [teaching index](../course-plan.md#6-twelve-dated-teaching-weeks)
and the agreed [clock timetable](../course-plan.md#4-timetable-and-vocabulary).

- Question: Can different histories fit the same available image evidence?
- Target: demonstrate that a defined evidence set cannot distinguish two
  histories, then specify what extra evidence could help.
- Lecture emphasis: week 3 controls a known process; this week starts with a
  limited observation and asks whether it determines the process. Define
  “indistinguishable” relative to what is available, not as a claim that two
  images must be byte-identical.
- Supplied pack: one source image, candidate route cards and anonymised outputs
  from two documented processing histories. Initially provide only thumbnails,
  dimensions and a stated observation tolerance. Prepare an evidence menu of
  evidence options; provide at least one actual item, such as a transformation-log
  excerpt, and mark unavailable items honestly. No platform accounts or uploads
  are required. Additional crops and intermediate downloads are optional.
- Investigate (40 minutes): construct two histories compatible with the initial
  observations (15); choose one additional evidence item and explain what result
  would discriminate between them (10); inspect that item, revise the conclusion
  and record anything still unresolved (15). Do not demand an origin guess.
- Output: ambiguity report containing two route diagrams, an observation table,
  the scope of indistinguishability, and a justified request for further evidence.
  Deposit both documented branches in the image archive; retain the source and
  logs for weeks 10–11. Reuse week 3's habit of explicitly defining the measure.
- Check: two route cards fit a supplied thumbnail; propose an available evidence
  item that could distinguish them and explain how. Feedback accepts an unresolved
  answer if the proposed evidence remains non-discriminating.
- Candidate reading: Hito Steyerl, “In Defense of the Poor Image.” Distinguish
  the cultural argument from technical claims that need separate evidence.
- Accessible route: descriptive feature tables expose the same staged evidence;
  the task tests inference rather than eyesight. Assessment link: add a bounded
  conclusion to The Chain and practise alternatives for The Stemma (LO3).
- Before publishing: verify that both routes really fit the initial observations
  at the declared tolerance. If they do not, revise the pack rather than asserting
  ambiguity. Check which extra evidence separates the routes and which does not.

## 3. How the lecture and workshop run

### Monday lecture — 60 minutes

Explain the inverse problem through an image example: an observed result may admit several histories. Define indistinguishability relative to the available observations and tolerance.

See the [shared lecture procedure](README.md#shared-teaching-and-page-procedure).

### Wednesday workshop — 120 minutes

**Investigation (40 minutes):** Use the staged 40-minute evidence-request exercise in the teaching brief. Two compatible routes must be proposed before extra evidence is revealed.

Use the workshop stages and quiz rubric from the
[shared procedure](README.md#shared-teaching-and-page-procedure).
