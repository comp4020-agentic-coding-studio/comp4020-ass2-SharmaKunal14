## 1. Purpose and progression

**Distinct capability:** Evaluate a restoration claim feature by feature against retained evidence.

**Archive placement:** Image: restoration comparison. Return to the image family from week 4; use its ambiguity lesson. Week 11 tests the record of the processing, which is distinct from whether restored detail matches the source.

## 2. Weekly teaching brief

Use week 10 dates from the [teaching index](../course-plan.md#6-twelve-dated-teaching-weeks)
and the agreed [clock timetable](../course-plan.md#4-timetable-and-vocabulary).

- Question: Does a convincing restoration match the original?
- Target: distinguish plausible detail, verified agreement and remaining ambiguity.
- Activity: compare week 4's degraded image, its retained source and one prepared
  restoration output. Identify specific features and assess correspondence;
  do not classify every newly computed pixel as fabricated information.
- Output: supported/unsupported/uncertain feature table in the image family.
- Check: evaluate one restoration claim using a three-image comparison. Feedback
  explains why visual appeal alone is insufficient evidence.
- Candidate reading: a primary restoration paper and one documented case study,
  selected and verified before drafting claims; PULSE is a possible case.
- Accessible route: enlarged labelled details and equivalent feature descriptions.
  Assessment link: required evaluation of a preservation/restoration claim in A3.

## 3. How the lecture and workshop run

### Monday lecture — 60 minutes

Separate visual plausibility, correspondence with a retained source and uncertainty. Work through supported/unsupported/uncertain features; do not label all newly computed pixels as invented content.

See the [shared lecture procedure](README.md#shared-teaching-and-page-procedure).

### Wednesday workshop — 120 minutes

**Investigation (40 minutes):** Examine the degraded/source/restoration comparisons (10 minutes); complete the feature table (20); test a claimed recovery and state a limitation (10). Use one prepared restoration output; a second is optional.

Use the workshop stages and quiz rubric from the
[shared procedure](README.md#shared-teaching-and-page-procedure).
